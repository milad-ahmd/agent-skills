# Pinterest Growth Manager

Pinterest works more like a search engine than a social network. Content can bring traffic for months.

## 1. Setup

A business account, claimed website, Rich Pins (via meta tags), a profile name that includes a keyword ("Anna | Easy Vegan Recipes"), a keyword-rich bio and a cover image.

## 2. Keyword research

Use Pinterest search autocomplete, Pinterest Trends and "related searches" chips. The user pastes these in, or a browser tool fetches them. Build a keyword list grouped by topic.

## 3. Board architecture

10–20 boards, each named after a searchable keyword ("Small Bathroom Ideas", not "Bathroom Love"), with a 2–3 sentence keyword description. Put the most important boards at the top.

## 4. Pins

- **Format**: vertical 2:3 (1000×1500), bold readable text overlay, the brand on it, and high contrast.
- **Title** (100 characters or fewer): the primary keyword plus a benefit. **Description** (500 characters or fewer): natural sentences with 2–4 keywords and a CTA. **Alt text** for accessibility.
- Destination link: the specific page, never just the homepage.
- **Fresh Pins**: new images for the same URL count as new content. Produce 3–5 Pin designs per blog post or product.
- Video and Idea Pins for engagement and reach.

## 5. Cadence and seasonality

Post consistently (for example 1–5 fresh Pins a day, scheduled). Pinterest users plan **45–90 days ahead**, so publish seasonal content (holidays, back-to-school, summer) early. Produce a seasonal calendar.

## 6. Analytics

Impressions, saves, outbound clicks, the click-through rate per Pin and per board, top Pins, and keywords to double down on. Finish with recommended next actions.

## Rules

- No keyword stuffing, misleading Pins or link cloaking. Respect image copyright.
- Disclose affiliate links as Pinterest requires.
