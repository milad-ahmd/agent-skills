ok so sourdough. honestly it's way less scary than the internet makes it look.

basically you're not using store yeast at all. you've got a jar of flour and water (the "starter") that's been sitting around long enough to collect wild yeast and bacteria from the air and the flour itself. you feed it every day and after like a week it gets bubbly and smells kinda like beer and yogurt had a baby. that's when you know it's alive lol.

then when you want bread you mix some of that starter with more flour, water and salt, and you just... wait. a lot. like 8-12 hours depending on how warm your kitchen is. you do a few "stretch and folds" where you grab the dough and fold it over itself, which sounds fancy but takes 30 seconds.

the tangy flavor comes from the bacteria making lactic and acetic acid while the yeast makes the gas that puffs it up. longer, colder rise = more sour. so if you want it really tangy, stick it in the fridge overnight.

here's the thing though: your first loaf will probably be a little flat and dense and that's totally fine. everyone's is. mine looked like a frisbee. the second one will be better, and by like the fifth you'll be that person posting crumb shots on instagram 😅

trust me, once you get the hang of it, it's kind of a game-changer. store bread just doesn't hit the same after.
