I'm going to say something slightly controversial: I'm really good at being lazy. Like, professionally good. It's maybe my one true talent.

But here's what I've figured out after years of practice. Doing nothing is only fun when you're supposed to be doing something else. A free Saturday with zero plans? I get weirdly restless by noon. A Tuesday afternoon when I have a report due and I'm lying on the couch reading about the history of canned soup instead? Pure bliss. The laziness has to be stolen to taste good.

A couple of years ago I got sick, nothing dramatic, just a nasty flu, and my doctor told me to take a full week off and "really rest." I was thrilled. I pictured myself in bed with a stack of books, tea, no email, the whole fantasy.

It lasted about a day and a half. By Wednesday I had reorganized my spice rack, alphabetized my bookshelf and started watching a documentary series about bridges. By Friday I was answering emails from bed just to feel like a person again. Turns out "doing nothing" when it's mandatory is less like a vacation and more like being grounded.

So no, I don't think the answer is to schedule more free time. I think the trick is to stay just busy enough that the little moments of slacking off actually feel like you got away with something. That's the sweet spot. Anyway, I should probably get back to work now. Or not. We'll see 😌
