composting is honestly so much easier than people think, you're basically just helping stuff rot faster lol

the main idea: you mix "greens" and "browns" in a pile or bin and let microbes eat it. greens are the wet, nitrogen-y stuff like veggie scraps, coffee grounds, fresh grass clippings. browns are the dry, carbony stuff like dead leaves, cardboard, shredded paper. a rough rule people use is like 2-3 parts browns to 1 part greens by volume but nobody's measuring, you'll eyeball it.

then it needs air and a bit of water. you want it about as damp as a wrung-out sponge. turn it with a pitchfork every week or two so the microbes get oxygen, otherwise it goes anaerobic and starts smelling like a swamp. if it stinks, add more browns. if nothing's happening, add more greens or water. that's like 90% of troubleshooting right there.

don't put in meat, dairy, or greasy stuff unless you want raccoons as roommates. also skip pet poop.

a hot, well-managed pile can be ready in a couple months. a lazy pile (mine) takes more like a year but it still gets there. you'll know it's done when it's dark, crumbly and smells like a forest floor instead of garbage.

then you just mix it into your garden beds and your plants go kind of feral in a good way 🌱
