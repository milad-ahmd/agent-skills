Since its launch in 1990, the Hubble Space Telescope has transformed our understanding of the universe in ways that few scientific instruments ever have. Orbiting about 340 miles above Earth, Hubble escapes the distortion of our atmosphere, allowing it to capture images of unprecedented clarity and detail.

Hubble's journey, however, did not begin smoothly. Shortly after launch, scientists discovered a flaw in its primary mirror that caused images to appear blurry. In 1993, astronauts aboard the Space Shuttle Endeavour carried out a daring servicing mission to install corrective optics, and the results were nothing short of spectacular. Over the following decades, four additional servicing missions upgraded its instruments and extended its lifespan far beyond original expectations.

The telescope's scientific contributions are truly staggering. Hubble helped astronomers determine the age of the universe with remarkable precision, provided key evidence for the existence of dark energy, and revealed that supermassive black holes likely reside at the centers of most galaxies. Its iconic images, from the "Pillars of Creation" to the Hubble Deep Field, have not only advanced science but also captured the public imagination.

Beyond its discoveries, Hubble represents the power of international collaboration, as it was developed jointly by NASA and the European Space Agency. It has inspired generations of scientists, students, and stargazers around the world.

As newer observatories like the James Webb Space Telescope take the stage, Hubble's legacy remains firmly secure. It stands as a testament to human curiosity, ingenuity, and our enduring desire to explore the cosmos.
