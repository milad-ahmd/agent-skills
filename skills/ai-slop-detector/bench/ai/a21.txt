Lightning is one of nature's most spectacular and dangerous phenomena. Here's a closer look at how it forms and how you can stay safe.

**How Lightning Forms**

Lightning occurs when electrical charges build up within a thunderstorm cloud. As ice particles and water droplets collide inside the cloud, they exchange charge, causing the upper part of the cloud to become positively charged and the lower part to become negatively charged. When the difference in charge becomes large enough to overcome the insulating properties of the air, a sudden discharge occurs, and that's lightning.

Lightning can strike within a cloud, between clouds, or between a cloud and the ground. A single bolt can heat the surrounding air to around 50,000°F, roughly five times hotter than the surface of the sun. This rapid heating causes the air to expand explosively, producing the sound we know as thunder.

**Lightning Safety Tips**

- **When thunder roars, go indoors:** If you can hear thunder, you are close enough to be struck.
- **Avoid open areas:** Stay away from fields, hilltops, and open water.
- **Don't shelter under trees:** Tall, isolated objects attract lightning.
- **Stay off corded phones and away from plumbing:** Electrical currents can travel through wiring and pipes.
- **Wait 30 minutes:** Remain indoors for at least 30 minutes after the last clap of thunder.

By understanding how lightning works and following these simple precautions, you can significantly reduce your risk and enjoy the beauty of a thunderstorm safely from indoors. Stay safe out there!
