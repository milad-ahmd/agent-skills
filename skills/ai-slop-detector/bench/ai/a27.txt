The Library of Congress, located in Washington, D.C., is the largest library in the world and one of the most significant cultural institutions in the United States. Established in 1800, it serves as the research arm of Congress while also offering an unparalleled wealth of resources to scholars, researchers, and the general public.

The Library's collection is truly staggering in scope. It holds more than 170 million items, including books, manuscripts, maps, photographs, sound recordings, and films in hundreds of languages. Among its most treasured holdings are a Gutenberg Bible, Thomas Jefferson's personal library, and rough drafts of the Declaration of Independence.

Beyond its role as a repository of knowledge, the Library of Congress plays a vital role in American public life. It houses the U.S. Copyright Office, which registers creative works, and the Congressional Research Service, which provides nonpartisan analysis to lawmakers. It also administers programs such as the National Book Festival and the Poet Laureate position, fostering a love of literature and learning across the nation.

In recent years, the Library has made significant strides in digitization, making millions of items available online. This commitment to accessibility ensures that people around the world, regardless of their location, can explore its vast collections.

Whether you are a historian, a student, or simply a curious visitor, the Library of Congress offers something for everyone. Its majestic Thomas Jefferson Building, with its stunning architecture and breathtaking Main Reading Room, is itself a testament to the enduring importance of knowledge in a democratic society.
